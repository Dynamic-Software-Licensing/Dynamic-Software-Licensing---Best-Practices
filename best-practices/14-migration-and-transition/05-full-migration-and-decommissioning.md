# 14.05. Full Migration and Decommissioning

## Purpose

This section defines the phase in which legacy licensing mechanisms are retired and the DSL
model becomes the primary operational baseline.

## Role of This Phase

The purpose of this phase is to complete the transition from coexistence to controlled primary
use of DSL-aligned licensing. It is where organizations deliberately remove dependency on
legacy servers, dongles, host-bound activation paths, or opaque manual processes.

## Typical Activities

- Identify which legacy components remain in use and why.
- Confirm that equivalent DSL-aligned lifecycle and operational capabilities are in place.
- Cut over the remaining workloads, products, or environments.
- Retire obsolete operational runbooks and update governance, support, and audit processes.
- Decommission or isolate legacy infrastructure that no longer has an operational role.

## Readiness Indicators

Full migration is usually credible only when:

- pilot and broader technical rollout have proven stable,
- lifecycle operations are operationally well understood,
- observability and audit expectations are satisfied,
- critical failover and continuity scenarios have been exercised,
- remaining legacy use is exceptional rather than baseline.

## Decommissioning Concerns

Decommissioning should account for:

- retained legal or contractual dependency on older licensing forms,
- archival or audit obligations,
- interoperability with vendors that have not yet completed their own transition,
- rollback boundaries if a remaining critical issue is discovered.

## Expected Outcomes

By the end of this phase, DSL-aligned licensing is the normal operating model and legacy
licensing paths have been reduced to explicitly managed exceptions or removed entirely.

## Section Positioning

This section represents the point at which DSL becomes the accepted baseline rather than a
transition program.
