# 14.06. Scaling and Industry Establishment

## Purpose

This section defines the phase in which DSL adoption moves beyond individual migrations and
becomes repeatable, scalable, and more broadly established across teams, products, and
industry participants.

## Role of This Phase

The final transition phase is not only about internal rollout. It is also about making the DSL
approach understandable, repeatable, and interoperable across a broader vendor and customer
ecosystem.

## Typical Activities

- Reuse the migration approach across additional products, services, and deployment profiles.
- Standardize internal implementation patterns, operational playbooks, and integration models.
- Align partner, reseller, integrator, and customer expectations around common lifecycle
 semantics and terminology.
- Improve supporting annexes, schemas, examples, and governance artifacts as adoption grows.
- Feed operational and commercial lessons back into future best-practices revisions.

## Scaling Characteristics

At this stage, the DSL model should support:

- repeated adoption with lower marginal effort,
- more predictable onboarding of new products or participants,
- stronger cross-vendor interoperability,
- clearer shared vocabulary and expectation setting,
- more stable review and publication cycles for the best practices themselves.

## Industry Establishment Considerations

Broader establishment requires more than technical reuse. It also depends on:

- controlled terminology,
- stable lifecycle semantics,
- credible migration examples,
- governance that can handle future extensions without fragmenting the model,
- enough implementation evidence to make the approach trustworthy to new adopters.

## Expected Outcomes

By the end of this phase, DSL is no longer treated as a one-off migration exercise. It has
become a repeatable operational and industry pattern that can continue to evolve through
versioned best-practices updates.

## Section Positioning

This section represents the transition from project-level adoption to ecosystem-level
normalization.
