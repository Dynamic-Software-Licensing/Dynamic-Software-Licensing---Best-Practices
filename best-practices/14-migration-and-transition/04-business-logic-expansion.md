# 14.04. Business Logic Expansion

## Purpose

This section defines the phase in which the commercial and business-logic capabilities of the
DSL model expand beyond simple technical replacement of legacy licensing.

## Role of This Phase

Early DSL adoption may focus primarily on operational improvement. Over time, however,
organizations and vendors typically want to use the model to support richer entitlement
segmentation, more flexible modalities, and more transparent usage-to-cost relationships.

This phase is where the model becomes commercially more capable without abandoning the
canonical lifecycle and trust semantics.

## Typical Activities

- Expand from a single dominant modality to multiple modality types where justified.
- Introduce clearer differentiation between production, test, event, or partner usage scope.
- Improve usage attribution and forecasting using richer dimensions and work correlation.
- Support more explicit self-service or delegated control for customers and integrators.
- Integrate licensing data into finance, procurement, analytics, or marketplace-style systems.

## Transition Considerations

This phase should not be treated as a purely commercial exercise. Business-logic growth must
stay aligned with:

- the canonical data model,
- the operational profiles,
- security and tenant-isolation constraints,
- the requirement that lifecycle semantics remain stable across modalities.

## Expected Outcomes

By the end of this phase, the DSL model should support a broader set of real business cases,
including more granular event licensing, richer usage-driven services, and clearer governance of
multi-product or multi-scope entitlement landscapes.

## Relationship to Later Phases

Business-logic expansion often exposes the final legacy constraints that still block full
cutover. It therefore provides the basis for eventual decommissioning and broader scaling.

## Section Positioning

This section represents the phase where DSL becomes not only technically viable but also
commercially and operationally expressive enough for broader adoption.
