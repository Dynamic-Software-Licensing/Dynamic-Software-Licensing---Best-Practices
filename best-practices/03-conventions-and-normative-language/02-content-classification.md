# 03.02 Content Classification

## Purpose

This section defines how repository content is classified so that readers can distinguish
between requirements, explanation, and supporting context.

Clear classification matters because the best practices combines architecture, requirements,
examples, profiles, annexes, and migration guidance in one coherent body of material.

## Content Classes

The document uses three practical content classes:
- normative content,
- informative content,
- explanatory or editorial support content.

## Normative Content

Normative content defines behavior, constraints, obligations, or interpretation rules that are
important for consistent implementation or assessment.

Normative content is usually found in:
- the normative requirements chapter,
- normative annexes,
- conformance interpretation passages,
- selected parts of security, API, and data model chapters where disciplined interpretation is
  necessary.

Normative content is written so that readers can identify what is being expected and why that
expectation matters.

## Informative Content

Informative content explains the model, gives context, illustrates choices, or helps readers
apply the best practices correctly.

Informative content may include:
- introduction and background material,
- explanatory architecture text,
- operational profiles,
- migration discussion,
- examples and mappings,
- informative annexes.

Informative text may strongly influence understanding, but it is not mistaken for a
requirement merely because it is useful or persuasive.

## Explanatory and Editorial Support Content

Some passages exist to improve readability, document structure, or local interpretation. These
include summaries, transition sentences, rationale, and limited editorial framing.

Such content is acceptable when it helps readers navigate the material, but it does not replace
substantive content and does not blur the distinction between guidance and requirement language.

## Handling Mixed Passages

Some sections contain a mix of requirement and explanation. In those cases:
- the requirement sentence is clearly identifiable,
- the explanatory sentence clarifies the rule rather than broadening it implicitly,
- examples remain recognizably illustrative,
- the structure avoids hiding the normative meaning inside long narrative prose.

If a reader cannot tell which part of a passage is normative, the wording needs improvement.

## Relationship to Chapter Types

Different chapters have different dominant content classes:
- Chapters 01, 02, 05, 06, 07, 13, 14, and 15 are primarily informative or interpretive.
- Chapter 09 is the primary home of normative requirements.
- Chapters 08, 10, 11, and 12 may contain a mix of interpretive and normative material,
  depending on the subject and the precision required.
- Normative annexes support disciplined interpretation and may restate or summarize normative
  material where that improves consistency.

## Section Positioning

This section is the classification guide for reading and reviewing the repository. It is used
whenever a passage needs to be evaluated for whether it states a requirement, explains a
requirement, or only provides supporting context.
