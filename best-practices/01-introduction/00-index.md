## Purpose

This chapter introduces the business and technical context behind the Dynamic Software Licensing best practices.

It explains why a new licensing approach is needed, why existing models are increasingly misaligned with modern broadcast and media workflows, and why the DSL initiative is relevant for the industry.

## Initiative Context

Dynamic Software Licensing (DSL) is an IABM initiative focused on modern software licensing for broadcast and media workflows.

This best-practices document is intended to support a more consistent and future-ready licensing approach across manufacturers, broadcasters, media organizations, system integrators, and other stakeholders.

## Industry Background

The broadcast and media industry is transitioning from hardware-bound and static deployment models toward software-defined, cloud-enabled, and hybrid operating environments.

As workflows become more dynamic, distributed, and automated, licensing can no longer remain a separate, manual, or infrastructure-external function. Licensing must evolve together with the services, platforms, and orchestration models it governs.

## Problem Context

Current software licensing approaches often create friction in modern environments.

Recurring challenges include:
- fragmented product and licensing models,
- poor portability between on-premises and cloud environments,
- manual setup, assignment, and renewal processes,
- limited alignment with DevOps, Infrastructure-as-Code, and CI/CD workflows,
- insufficient transparency for usage, cost, and forecasting,
- limited auditability and incomplete security integration.

These issues increase complexity for both vendors and customers and slow down the transition toward more agile and service-oriented operating models.

## Why These Best Practices Exist

The DSL best practices exist to provide a shared framework for a more modern and interoperable licensing approach.

The intention is to support a licensing model that is:
- implementation-oriented,
- secure,
- observable,
- portable,
- automation-friendly,
- suitable for both continuous and event-driven production scenarios.

## Role of This Chapter

This chapter is not intended to define detailed requirements.

Its role is to:
- establish the industry context,
- summarize the problem space,
- explain the motivation for the DSL initiative,
- create a common starting point for the remainder of the best practices.
