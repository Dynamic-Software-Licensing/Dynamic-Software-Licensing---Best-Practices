# 03. The DSL Initiative

## Origin

A review of IAMT member organizations in the vendor community identified a clear gap: there is no common approach to software licensing within the broadcast and media industry.

Each manufacturer implements licensing differently. Each activation process is proprietary. System integrators building multi-vendor solutions must navigate a different licensing model for every product in the stack, and customers bear the accumulated cost of that fragmentation in operational overhead, audit risk, and deployment friction.

The Dynamic Software Licensing (DSL) initiative was started in response to this finding. Rather than leaving the industry to develop further siloed, vendor-specific solutions in parallel, the initiative brings key stakeholders together to develop a shared framework of best practices.

## Purpose

The DSL initiative is a collaborative effort to define how broadcast and media software should be licensed in cloud-enabled, hybrid, and software-defined production environments.

Its output is not a formal technical standard with mandatory compliance obligations. It is a framework of best practices: a set of agreed principles, patterns, and reference approaches that manufacturers, customers, and system integrators can adopt to achieve more consistent, interoperable, and operationally sound licensing outcomes.

The initiative is deliberately implementation-oriented. The goal is practical adoption, not theoretical completeness.

## Participants

The DSL initiative operates under the IAMT — the International Association of MediaTech — and involves collaboration across multiple stakeholder groups:

- **Cloud platform providers**, who contributed architectural patterns and reference implementations
- **System integrators**, who provided operational requirements from real-world multi-vendor deployments
- **Independent software vendors (ISVs)**, who contributed requirements from the perspective of licensing system implementors
- **Broadcast customers and media organizations**, who articulated deployment, automation, and operational continuity requirements

This multi-stakeholder composition is intentional. Licensing challenges manifest differently depending on where in the ecosystem an organization sits, and a framework that only reflects the priorities of one constituency will not achieve broad adoption.

## Scope of the Initiative

The DSL initiative focuses on the licensing lifecycle for software applications running in broadcast and media production environments.

This includes how licenses are issued, renewed, moved across infrastructure, revoked, and metered. It covers the interfaces between licensing systems and the surrounding operational infrastructure — identity providers, orchestration platforms, infrastructure-as-code tooling, and observability systems.

It does not prescribe vendor pricing models, commercial terms, or customer billing arrangements. Those remain entirely within the scope of individual vendor-customer relationships.

## Why Collaboration Is the Right Approach

Individual vendors solving this problem in isolation will produce more fragmentation, not less.

A system integrator deploying ten different broadcast applications from ten different vendors cannot realistically maintain ten incompatible licensing integration paths in a single automated pipeline. A broadcaster operating a hybrid environment cannot govern licensing consistently if each product responds differently to failover events, network interruptions, or infrastructure changes.

Common best practices reduce the integration surface, lower the operational burden on all parties, and give the entire ecosystem a shared foundation to build on. The value of the framework increases as more of the industry adopts it.

IAMT members are driving this initiative, and its success depends on continued industry collaboration and a shared commitment to developing and accepting a common approach to licensing.
